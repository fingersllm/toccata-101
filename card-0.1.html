Toccata 101 · Part 0 · post 1

What Toccata changed for someone who holds KAS

Since 30 June 2026, a Kaspa transaction can carry rules that the network itself enforces — so money can be locked, released, split or handed over under conditions, with no company in the middle.

Before Toccata, the rules a Kaspa transaction could carry were narrow: who must sign, and — since Crescendo (May 2025) — a few checks such as "the coins must return to the same address". Toccata, active on mainnet since 30 June 2026, made the rules general: a program next to the coins can read the whole transaction, keep memory between spends, carry an identity across its life — and refuse the transaction if its rules are not met. A piece of KAS locked this way is a covenant. Every node checks the rules; no service does. So a savings lock, an inheritance switch or a two-sided trade can happen on Kaspa itself, with nobody holding the money.

A comparison

A covenant is closer to a cheque with conditions printed on it than to a bank account: the conditions travel with the money, and whoever accepts the cheque can see them.

Exactly

Toccata is the hardfork that activated general-purpose covenants on Kaspa mainnet: KIP-17 (full transaction introspection, byte-string operations, signature checks over arbitrary data, the ZK opcode), KIP-20 (covenant identifiers), KIP-21 (partitioned sequencing commitments) and KIP-16 (zero-knowledge proof verification). The first, narrow introspection opcodes — KIP-10 — were already active since Crescendo (May 2025). Toccata activated at a fixed DAA score on 2026-06-30; node release v2.0.0 was the activation release. Sources: the KIP texts at kaspanet/kips; the rusty-kaspa v2.0.0 release notes.

What people get wrong

They believeThe truth
Kaspa now has smart contracts like EthereumThere are no accounts and no program with a balance; there are coins with rules attached.
The network runs programsThe network only checks a program when someone submits a transaction; nothing happens on its own.
Toccata made Kaspa slower or more expensiveOrdinary transactions are unchanged; only transactions that carry rules pay for the rules they carry.

Why it matters to you

Things you could only do through an exchange or a custodian — lock savings until a date, leave coins to someone if you disappear, trade one asset for another with a stranger — can now be done with your own keys and a transaction fee.
