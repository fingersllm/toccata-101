Toccata 101 · Part 0 · card 1

What Toccata changed for someone who holds KAS

Since 30 June 2026, a Kaspa transaction can carry rules that the network itself enforces. Money can be locked, released, split or handed over under conditions.

Before Toccata, the rules a Kaspa transaction could carry were narrow. Mostly just moving coins from one wallet to another using the PoW, UTXO model. The Crescendo hardfork let a rule look inside its own transaction: count the inputs and outputs, check the amount and the address of each. Still, a rule couldn't keep memory from one spend to the next, or carry an identity across spends.

Toccata made the rules general. A program next to the coins can read the whole transaction, keep memory between spends, carry an identity across its life, and refuse the transaction if its rules are not met. The network nodes verify all of this. A piece of KAS locked this way is a covenant. This opens interesting options for what you can do with Layer 1 coins and assets.

Long story short

On Toccata, its possible to have rules travel with money: KAS coins and L1 assets. The rules enforce themself or refuses by the network nodes.

Sources

Toccata is the hardfork that activated general covenants on Kaspa mainnet: KIP-17 (full transaction introspection, byte-string operations, signature checks over arbitrary data, the ZK opcode), KIP-20 (covenant identifiers), KIP-21 (partitioned sequencing commitments) and KIP-16 (zero-knowledge proof verification). The first, narrow introspection opcodes, KIP-10, were already live since Crescendo (May 2025). Toccata activated at a fixed DAA score on 30 June 2026. Node release v2.0.0 (5 June 2026) was the activation release. The KIP texts are at github.com/kaspanet/kips.

Misconceptions

AppearsThe truth
Kaspa now has smart contractsThere are no accounts and no program holding a balance. There are coins with rules attached.
The network runs programsThe network only checks a program when someone submits a transaction
A covenant on Kaspa is a one trick ponyA covenant can decide who may spend, when spending is allowed, where the coins go and in what amounts, what the next output must look like, and what else must be in the same transaction. Put together, that is enough for savings locks, trades and tokens.

New possibilities

Things you could only do through an exchange or a custodian, like lock savings until a date, leave coins to someone if you disappear, trade one asset for another with a stranger and much more, can now be done with your own keys and a transaction fee.
